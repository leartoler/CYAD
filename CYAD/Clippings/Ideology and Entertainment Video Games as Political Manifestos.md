The gaming community has been debating for decades whether video games can be “art.” This debate seems naive and outdated today. The much more pressing and urgent question that confronts us is not the aesthetic value of games, but their ideological function. The widespread myth of the “apolitical” nature of games—the claim that they are purely escapist entertainment—is now seen not just as false. It is itself a political position aimed at preserving the status quo and obscuring the mechanisms of influence.

Modern video games are not just fun entertainment and interesting stories. They are system simulators, experience architects, and, most importantly, decision-making simulators. They have evolved into a full-fledged platform for political influence.

We have already touched on this topic in the article [Games are politics. How the Russian game industry helped prepare this war](https://mezha.ua/en/articles/games-are-politics-how-the-russian-game-industry-helped-prepare-this-war/). In this text, we will try to understand how games broadcast political manifestos, why developers consciously choose this path, and what consequences this has for public consciousness.

## Influence methodology

The unique interactive nature of video games makes them the most powerful ideological communication tool of the 21st century, capable of not just showing but of forcing the player to experience and internalize a political idea. Unlike passive media such as film or literature, where the person is simply an observer, video games require participation. This fundamental shift from observation to action is key.

The term "procedural rhetoric," [coined by](https://direct.mit.edu/books/monograph/4392/Persuasive-GamesThe-Expressive-Power-of-Videogames) Professor Ian Bogost, is central to our analysis. It means "the art of persuasion through rules of conduct, through the representation of processes."

[![Screenshot from the game Tom Clancy's The Division 2](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/9/8/285114/984068173cc9876612a94971173e20c01763116573.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/9/8/285114/984068173cc9876612a94971173e20c01763116573.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Tom Clancy's The Division 2

In other words, a game's ideological message is often embedded not so much in its plot, dialogue, or visual style, although these are also there, as they are a more understandable level of perception for the masses, but in its mechanics and game cycles. The game convinces the player by forcing him to act according to certain rules and see the consequences of those actions, which shapes his understanding of the system.

A game gives the player agency—the ability to act in a virtual world. However, this agency is always limited by the rules set by the developer. By accepting these rules, the player becomes complicit in the ideology embedded in the game. When you choose to “save” or “kill” a fictional character, you don’t just watch the hero do it. You make the choice yourself. This creates an “imposed experience” that has powerful empathic and cognitive impact.

[![Screenshot from the game Mass Effect 2](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/a/5/285115/a52befc2231a22d0e69edd92e84685471763116620.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/a/5/285115/a52befc2231a22d0e69edd92e84685471763116620.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Mass Effect 2

There are [scientific studies](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563224002929) that prove that the moral decisions we make inside video games are not limited to the virtual space or the conditional "vacuum" of a particular game. Our morality in games is directly related to morality in the real world. And this creates a huge field for experiments with the consciousness of players and attempts to implement specific ideas into it.

Psychologically, the player does not simply control the game character; he embodies it. In a first- or third-person game, the player's brain begins to perceive the actions of the virtual character as his own. This phenomenon of "embodied cognition" means that a political act performed in the game, such as affixing a stamp in [Papers, Please](https://papersplea.se/), is internalized at a deep cognitive level. The player learns ideology through muscle memory and reflexive actions, not just through intellectual reasoning.

[![Screenshot from the game Vampyr](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/2/6/285116/2682bf3917ccf5fee314c26ab5ce43e71763116646.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/2/6/285116/2682bf3917ccf5fee314c26ab5ce43e71763116646.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Vampyr

A game can simulate complex political or economic systems, allowing the player to "live" them. This is not an analysis of the system from the outside, not an exploration of it through the eyes of another author, but an assimilation of it from the inside. Thus, the game becomes not just a commentary, but a simulated argument. When game mechanics and plot work in harmony, creating ludonarrative coherence, the ideological message is amplified many times over.

It is through these mechanisms that the game is capable of deep, often subconscious, ideological transmission. Now let's look at how this works in practice.

## Visual examples

Let's analyze examples that demonstrate different methods of incorporating political manifestos – from direct authorial statements to the hidden ideology of system simulators.

### BioShock: A Critique of Radical Individualism

[![Art for the game Bioshock](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/4/9/285117/49d4cdd0d04d4e231dd5f2f7a41611cc1763116693.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/4/9/285117/49d4cdd0d04d4e231dd5f2f7a41611cc1763116693.jpg)

Art for the game Bioshock

Ken Levine's [BioShock](https://2k.com/games/bioshock/bioshock-1/) is a devastating critique of Ayn Rand's objectivism and unregulated libertarianism. It's a manifesto against the idea of a "creator-man" who rejects the social contract. The game conveys this not through direct dialogue, although it can't do without it, but through world design and game mechanics.

The underwater city of Rapture is not just a set piece. It is a ruined utopia, the physical embodiment of Andrew Ryan’s ideology, rotten from within. The Art Deco architecture, combining opulence with widespread decay, makes a visual argument that an ideology that ignores the weak inevitably devours itself.

The mechanics of genetic modification, plasmids, are a metaphor for an unregulated market where the body becomes a commodity. This leads to social collapse and the literal dehumanization of the population.

The game's main moral choice - to "save" or "devour" the girls for the ADAM resource - is a direct attack on Objectivist ethics. The game forces the player to choose between self-interest and altruism, with altruism proving more beneficial in the long run, which is a direct polemic with Rand.

The famous plot twist "Would you kindly?" is a commentary not only on the plot, but also on the very nature of the player, who follows commands, believing in his free will. It is a critique of blind faith in any imposed system, including ideological ones.

### Papers, Please. A simulation of totalitarian bureaucracy

[![Screenshot from the game Papers, Please](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/7/5/285118/759d6a2147a27b452d03ad0deb2a5e801763116731.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/7/5/285118/759d6a2147a27b452d03ad0deb2a5e801763116731.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Papers, Please

Lucas Pope's work is a profound analysis of the "banality of evil." It is a manifesto about how ordinary people become instruments of a repressive system through bureaucracy and dehumanization. [Papers, Please](https://papersplea.se/) is a perfect example of procedural rhetoric.

The main cycle of the game is work. The mechanics are to compare documents, find discrepancies, and stamp "Approved" or "Denyed." This monotonous, stressful activity is the message. The player physically "enacts" an act of bureaucratic violence.

The player doesn't deal with "people," he deals with "documents," which imposes a dehumanization. The system deliberately forces the player to see the pixel characters not as individuals with stories, but as a set of papers to be checked.

Additionally, the player is forced to be a "useful cog" in the system to earn money for food and medicine for his own family. Moral dilemmas of the level of "letting through a person with fake documents who is begging for rescue" become a luxury, because a mistake is punished. The game skillfully models the ethics of collaborationism.

### This War of Mine. A Humanist Anti-War Manifesto

[![Screenshot from the game This War of Mine](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/e/f/285120/efe8d064bc7a642b7fa07c46cac13dec1763116785.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/e/f/285120/efe8d064bc7a642b7fa07c46cac13dec1763116785.jpg)

Screenshot from the game This War of Mine

[This War of Mine](https://www.thiswarofmine.com/) is a radical deconstruction of the "war game" genre. It's a manifesto that war is not just about the heroism of soldiers on the battlefield, but also about the unspeakable suffering of civilians.

The player controls not a soldier, but a group of civilians hiding in a dilapidated house. Instead of "killing the enemy", the goal is to "find food" or "get medicine". The game cycle is divided into two phases: daytime shelter construction and nighttime raids for supplies.

The game constantly presents the player with a choice where there is no conventionally good option. Will you rob an elderly couple to save your own child from starvation? Will you stop sexual assault at the risk of getting shot? The game does not condemn, but mechanically shows the consequences: the characters fall into depression, become incapacitated. The message is broadcast through the emotional state of the characters controlled by the player. This is a direct procedural criticism of militarism.

### Call of Duty: The Unconscious Manifesto of Interventionism

[![Screenshot from the game Call of Duty: Black Ops](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/0/9/285121/09e0b6d40a46deb0c1d70c9f97281db91763116822.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/0/9/285121/09e0b6d40a46deb0c1d70c9f97281db91763116822.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Call of Duty: Black Ops

Unlike previous examples, this is an implicit but extremely influential manifesto. [The Call of Duty](https://www.callofduty.com/) series, especially the Modern Warfare era, is an ideological apparatus that normalizes American military interventionism, exports the concept of "just" preventive war, and dehumanizes the "enemy."

Enemies in Call of Duty are not people, but "targets" running around the corner. The game cycle, in which almost everything unfolds very quickly, with explosions and spectacular scenes, leaves no room for reflection. This is an exercise in animal reflex, not moral choice.

The player is always on the "right" side - the clearly defined, technologically superior one. The enemy is presented as an amorphous, irrational terrorist threat.

The infamous mission in Modern Warfare 2 involving the killing of Russians at an airport is less a criticism than an exploitation of terrorism for the sake of "shock" content, which ultimately only reinforces the narrative that extreme military measures are necessary to combat such evil.

### Spec Ops: The Line. Deconstructing the Militarist Player

[![Screenshot from the game Spec Ops: The Line](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/9/3/285122/93bae0b9fb40f2f583b99aa98706bc301763116848.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/9/3/285122/93bae0b9fb40f2f583b99aa98706bc301763116848.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Spec Ops: The Line

This game is a direct manifesto response to the ideology of Call of Duty. It is a critique not so much of war as of the portrayal of war in the media and of the player himself, who enjoys violence, even if unconsciously.

[Spec Ops: The Line](https://2k.com/games/spec-ops-the-line/) deliberately mimics a typical military shooter: gunfights, cover, pathetic dialogues, brave US soldiers as the main characters. However, gradually the player understands that the actions that the game pushes him to have terrible consequences. And the enemies around are the same US military, whose militaristic ideals have been reduced to absolutes.

The white phosphorus scene is a key moment. The game gives the player a drone as a tool and a cluster of enemies as a target. It soon becomes apparent that the player has killed civilians – and in a brutal way. The game has forced the player to commit a war crime and then framed them for it. It's a direct attack on the "ludo-narrative dissonance" of Call of Duty, where killing civilians is simply "game over" and a quick restart.

Even the loading screens of Spec Ops: The Line gradually change from "advice" to direct accusations of the player ("How many Americans have you killed today?", "Do you feel like a hero?"). It's a direct political statement about the responsibility of the consumer of media violence.

Of course, there are nuances: Spec Ops: The Line sacrifices player agency in order to convey its ideas, as scenes with the shooting of civilians are unavoidable. This is a debatable point that shows how the shortcomings of some games can become tools in others. Or even how the shortcomings of the systems in a particular game can reveal themselves from a new perspective over time.

### Disco Elysium. A Treatise on Ideological Collapse

[![Screenshot from the game Disco Elysium](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/c/1/285124/c1a0c3948481705a2c1a4c3a6f423d3e1763116884.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/c/1/285124/c1a0c3948481705a2c1a4c3a6f423d3e1763116884.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Disco Elysium

[Disco Elysium](https://discoelysium.com/) is a philosophical reflection on the death of the "great" ideologies of the 20th century (communism, fascism, liberalism) and on the deeply traumatized, fragmented modern personality.

The Thought mechanic turns ideologies into inventory items. The player can "think" about ideas, such as "Communism" or "Ultraliberalism." This takes game time, after which the thought is "internalized," granting the player certain bonuses and penalties to skills. It's a procedural metaphor for how ideology shapes our personalities and affects everything in life.

All 24 skills (Empathy, Logic, Authority, etc.) are fully-fledged characters who constantly argue in the protagonist's mind. This is a metaphorical reflection of a politically and socially fragmented society where it is impossible to find a single truth.

The game does not offer a "correct" ideology and a "correct" solution. Each of the paths is presented in a grotesque, bankrupt form. The political manifesto of the game is a manifesto of disappointment in political systems themselves, a fixation of the loneliness and hopelessness of a person in the midst of the "grayness" of life.

### Detroit: Become Human. A civil rights allegory

[![Screenshot from the game Detroit: Become Human](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/8/b/285125/8b600ef7b08f3accad92cef529c194bc1763117284.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/8/b/285125/8b600ef7b08f3accad92cef529c194bc1763117284.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Detroit: Become Human

[Detroit: Become Human](https://www.quanticdream.com/en/detroit-become-human) is a direct commentary on the history of the struggle for civil rights, the abolition of slavery, against xenophobia, and for minority rights through the prism of the relationship between humans and androids.

The game is extremely straightforward in its presentation. It visualizes each choice as a branching flowchart. This mechanizes the act of political choice, turning the story into a set of discrete nodes.

Detroit: Become Human doesn't resort to subtle metaphors, which of course won't appeal to everyone, but this approach makes it as accessible as possible. It shows androids standing in the back of a bus, separate areas for androids, police violence. It's a direct use of imagery from the American civil rights movement of the 1960s, which makes the manifesto accessible, but at the same time somewhat simplistic.

The player, controlling the leader of the uprising, Marcus, is forced to choose between peaceful protest in the spirit of Martin Luther King or violent rebellion in the spirit of Malcolm X. The game simulates this fundamental dilemma of social movements.

### Stellaris. Political Systems Laboratory

[![Screenshot from the game Stellaris](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/7/d/285126/7d02ecbf4688e8c043ecbdc3c42019091763117313.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/7/d/285126/7d02ecbf4688e8c043ecbdc3c42019091763117313.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Stellaris

The genre of global strategy (4X) is inherently political. [Stellaris](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/stellaris/) is a manifesto-simulator that argues that politics and ideology are constructs that can and even must be designed.

At the start of the game, the player literally constructs the ideology of their civilization, choosing "ethics" (e.g., xenophobia, pacifism, authoritarianism, materialism) and "civic models" (e.g., technocracy or slave-owning guilds).

The game doesn’t tell the player that genocide is bad. Instead, it shows the systemic consequences: destabilization, diplomatic crises, internal uprisings. Or, conversely, it can show that a totalitarian system is economically efficient, at least in the short term, forcing the player to make utilitarian, rather than moral, choices. This is the purest form of procedural rhetoric.

### Road 96. Travel and escape as a political act

[![Screenshot from the game Road 96](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/0/2/285127/0258d95b5c260004d875094d255bc78e1763117488.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/0/2/285127/0258d95b5c260004d875094d255bc78e1763117488.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Road 96

[Road 96](https://www.mobygames.com/game/169445/road-96/) is a commentary on the problems of modern youth, political apathy, class inequality, authoritarianism, and the choice between emigration, struggle, or conformity.

The plot is made up of random stories during a journey to the border. This creates the feeling of a fragmented, unstable world, where personal fate depends on chance. The very mechanics of fleeing the country, the journey to the titular Route 96, is also a political act.

Dialogues and small actions, such as vandalism or donations, affect the nationwide "election scale." The game conveys the idea that even small actions have political consequences.

## Why do developers do all this?

Understanding how games work and what they want to tell us leads us to the next logical question – why do studios and creators deliberately politicize their products?

For many independent developers, like Lucas Pope or ZA/UM, games are a form of personal expression. It's their way of leaving a mark on the culture, of commenting on the world they live in. It's a pure act of authorship.

Developers, like any artist, increasingly feel a responsibility to influence public discourse. 11 bit studios, the creators of This War of Mine, have openly stated their desire to change the discourse on war. Words have not gone unnoticed, so the company has regularly [donated money to help Ukraine](https://mezha.ua/en/2024/07/12/polish-11-bit-studios-donates-50-thousand-for-children-treated-at-okhmatdyt-this-is-a-share-of-the-profits-from-the-russian-game-indika/) during the Russian aggression.

[![Statement from the developers of This War of Mine regarding the Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/1/2/285128/12955054fb41a8a9bec33e1f297489af1763117560.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/1/2/285128/12955054fb41a8a9bec33e1f297489af1763117560.jpg)

Statement from the developers of This War of Mine regarding the Russian invasion of Ukraine

However, it is not only about morality and values, because video games are a business that has to make money. The commercial aspect cannot be ignored either. Political controversy generates attention, press and virality. So sometimes it is a conscious strategy.

The most insidious motive is the position of large corporations like Ubisoft, which for years have exploited political themes (neocolonialism, religious fanaticism, authoritarianism in the US), while paradoxically declaring their “apoliticalness.” This is also a political position – a position of neoliberal centrism, which seeks to avoid sharp corners, satisfy all sides of the market and, as a result, maintains the existing status quo.

[![Screenshot from the game Metal Gear Rising: Revengeance](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/2/d/285129/2d62fd690c309411c23009061ca586a61763117578.jpg?w=1920&q=90&f=webp)](https://img.mezha.ua/mezhaprod/system/MediaPhoto/photo/2/d/285129/2d62fd690c309411c23009061ca586a61763117578.jpg)

Screenshot from the game Metal Gear Rising: Revengeance

In the AAA industry, the ideology of the author, such as Hideo Kojima, often struggles with corporate demands. Games like Metal Gear Solid become "Trojan horses" that carry complex anti-war and anti-authoritarian ideas inside a commercial blockbuster. And it is a great art to lead your own ideas through all the circles of corporate approval hell.

## Ethical aspects and risks

The politicization of games also carries significant risks that should be kept in mind even by those who are against the apolitical nature of interactive entertainment.

**Risk of simplification.** Complex socio-political issues can be reduced to binary choices in a flowchart. Gamification of politics risks turning deep issues into entertainment, stripping them of real weight.

**Polarization and Culture Wars.** Gaming is becoming another battleground in the culture wars. Any attempt to incorporate inclusive or progressive themes immediately provokes a backlash from a segment of the gaming community that seeks to maintain a perceived apolitical status quo. Hence the constant [criticism of woke culture](https://mezha.ua/en/articles/a-conspiracy-theory-in-the-gaming-industry-western-game-developers-are-accused-of-promoting-woke-values-what-is-sweet-baby-inc-and-what-is-going-on/) and the further polarization of entertainment that should ideally unite people.

**Echo chambers and confirmation of biases.** Games, especially those that model ideologies or offer choices, may not challenge the player, but instead create a closed echo chamber where the player simply confirms their own political views, receiving positive reinforcement from the system.

**State propaganda and recruitment.** There is a direct danger of using procedural rhetoric for overt state propaganda. Military-sponsored games, such as [America's Army](https://www.mobygames.com/game/7640/americas-army-operations/), are direct recruitment tools. Totalitarian regimes can use game mechanics to spread their version of history or discredit opponents.

Art from the game Fallout: New Vegas

The claim that "games are outside politics" has been definitively discredited. It is as absurd as the claim that literature or cinema are outside politics. Every choice a designer makes – from the skin color of the protagonist to the rules of the game's economic system – is a political act, whether conscious or not.

Video games have not simply become political manifestos. They always have been, they have simply reached a level of narrative and mechanical complexity that is impossible to ignore. They have transformed from entertainment into a full-fledged political arena where ideologies are simulated, tested, and disseminated. We see how overt manifestos confront covert ones, how deconstructive games force us to reflect on the medium itself.

The prognosis for the future is clear: as the medium matures, its political voice will only become stronger and more expressive. The main challenge for us as a society is not to prohibit or condemn this politicization. We need to develop in ourselves and in future generations a critical game literacy – the ability to read, deconstruct, and understand the procedural rhetoric through which games communicate to us.

We must learn to see ideology not only in text or phrases, but also in the rules of the game itself, at all its levels.
