**Pokémon Yellow Version: Special Pikachu Edition** (Japanese: **ポケットモンスター　ピカチュウ** *Pocket Monsters Pikachu*), often known simply as **Pokémon Yellow Version**, is the third Pokémon game for [Game Boy](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") released worldwide, as a [solitary version](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Core_series "Core series") of [Pokémon Red and Blue Versions](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Blue_Versions "Pokémon Red and Blue Versions"). In Japan, the game was the fourth Pokémon game released, as a second solitary version of [Pokémon Red and Green](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Green_Versions "Pokémon Red and Green Versions"). Unlike other games, Pokémon Yellow was inspired by [*Pokémon the Series*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series "Pokémon the Series").

Similar to Red and Blue, Yellow arrived towards the end of the Game Boy's lifespan. It managed, though, to receive the title of second best-selling non-bundled game for its console, losing only to its predecessors.

The game was available on the [Nintendo 3DS](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nintendo_3DS "Nintendo 3DS") [Virtual Console](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Virtual_Console#Nintendo_3DS "Virtual Console") between the [Pokémon 20th Anniversary](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_20th_Anniversary "Pokémon 20th Anniversary") on [February 27, 2016](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Day#2016 "Pokémon Day") and the discontinuation of the 3DS Nintendo eShop on March 27, 2023.

[Generation VII](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_VII "Generation VII") [remakes](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Remakes "Remakes"), titled [Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon:_Let%27s_Go,_Pikachu!_and_Let%27s_Go,_Eevee! "Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!"), were released worldwide for the [Nintendo Switch](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nintendo_Switch "Nintendo Switch") in November 2018, in celebration of Yellow's 20th anniversary in Japan.

## Plot

**[Spoiler warning:](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bulbapedia:Spoilers "Bulbapedia:Spoilers")** this article may contain major plot or ending details.

The plot of Yellow is very similar to that of the Japanese [Red and Green](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Green_Versions "Pokémon Red and Green Versions") and [Japanese Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Blue_Version_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Blue Version (Japanese)") as well as of the [Western Red and Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Blue_Versions "Pokémon Red and Blue Versions"): the [player](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Player_character "Player character") ([canonically](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Canon "Canon") known as [Red](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Red_\(game\) "Red (game)")) begins his game in [Pallet Town](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pallet_Town "Pallet Town"), a small town in the [Kanto](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto "Kanto") region, on the same day as his former best friend and now [rival](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blue_\(game\) "Blue (game)").

While looking for [Professor Oak](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Professor_Oak "Professor Oak") who had called both of them to his laboratory, the player tries to leave town, only for Oak to catch up to him two steps into [Route 1](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_1 "Kanto Route 1") 's grassy path. Warning the player about the dangers of walking in [tall grass](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Tall_grass "Tall grass") without a Pokémon, Oak himself encounters a wild [Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Yellow\) "Pikachu (Yellow)") and manages to catch it, before leading the player to his laboratory, where he allows the player to take the single [Poké Ball](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Ball_\(item\) "Poké Ball (item)") containing an [Eevee](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Eevee_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Eevee (Pokémon)") from the desk near him. The [rival](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rival "Rival") quickly gets jealous, shoves the player out of his way and takes the Pokémon for himself. With no other options, Oak gives player the freshly caught Pikachu, after which the rival challenges the player to a [battle](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_battle "Pokémon battle").

After the battle, Oak allows the two new Trainers to leave for their [journey](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_journey "Pokémon journey") across [Kanto](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto "Kanto"). Right after the rival leaves, the player's Pikachu breaks out of his Poké Ball and [starts following the player on foot](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Walking_Pok%C3%A9mon "Walking Pokémon"). Stopping in [Viridian City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Viridian_City "Viridian City") 's [Poké Mart](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Mart "Poké Mart"), the player will find that a [package](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Parcel "Parcel") has come in for the professor, and the clerk asks that it be delivered to him. After this has been completed the professor gives one [Pokédex](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9dex "Pokédex") to the player and one to the rival, and sends them on their way. Viridian City has a Gym; however, it is locked.

From here, the player has his first encounter with other Trainers, on [Route 2](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_2 "Kanto Route 2") and in [Viridian Forest](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Viridian_Forest "Viridian Forest"), and his first encounter with a [Gym Leader](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gym_Leader "Gym Leader"): [Brock](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Brock "Brock") the [Rock-type](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rock_\(type\) "Rock (type)") Gym Leader of [Pewter City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pewter_City "Pewter City"). After his defeat, journeying along [Route 3](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_3 "Kanto Route 3") and through [Mt. Moon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mt._Moon "Mt. Moon") brings the player face to face with the regional [villainous team](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Villainous_team "Villainous team"), [Team Rocket](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Team_Rocket "Team Rocket"), who are attempting to extract rare [Fossils](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fossil "Fossil") from the cave. Their defeat allows the player to continue through the cave and obtain the [Dome Fossil](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Dome_Fossil "Dome Fossil") or [Helix Fossil](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Helix_Fossil "Helix Fossil") which can be regenerated into [Kabuto](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kabuto_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Kabuto (Pokémon)") and [Omanyte](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Omanyte_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Omanyte (Pokémon)"). Before the player leaves the cave, he is ambushed by two Team Rocket grunts (unnamed in this game but known as [Jessie](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jessie "Jessie") and [James](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/James "James") in [*Pokémon the Series*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series "Pokémon the Series")), who try to take the fossil back. Continuing onto [Route 4](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_4 "Kanto Route 4"), the player finds himself in [Cerulean City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cerulean_City "Cerulean City"), where another [Gym](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gym "Gym") is. This one, however, is run by [Misty](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Misty "Misty"), and specializes in [Water-type](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Water_\(type\) "Water (type)") Pokémon. To the north, as well, there are two [routes](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Route "Route") leading up to [Bill](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bill "Bill") 's cottage. On the way, the player is confronted with a trainer who tries to persuade him to join [Team Rocket](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Team_Rocket "Team Rocket"). When the player reaches [Bill](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bill "Bill") 's cottage and frees him of his transformation into a Pokémon, he will give the player a ticket for the [S.S. Anne](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/S.S._Anne "S.S. Anne"), a luxury ship moored in [Vermilion Harbor](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Vermilion_City#Port "Vermilion City") and filled with Trainers. Taking a shortcut through a house burglarized by Team Rocket, the player finally arrives at [Route 5](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_5 "Kanto Route 5").

After traveling down Routes 5 and [6](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_6 "Kanto Route 6"), using the [Underground Path](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Underground_Path_\(Kanto_Routes_5%E2%80%936\) "Underground Path (Kanto Routes 5–6)") to bypass [Saffron City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Saffron_City "Saffron City"), the player finally arrives in [Vermilion](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Vermilion_City "Vermilion City"). This city is home to another Pokémon Gym; however, the way to it is blocked by a small tree. The only thing to do is to show the ticket to the [Sailor](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Sailor_\(Trainer_class\) "Sailor (Trainer class)") guarding the harbor, allowing entry into the S.S. Anne. It is here, after assisting the captain with his seasickness, that the player will obtain the first of the five [Hidden Machines](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/HM "HM") available in the game, containing [Cut](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cut_\(move\) "Cut (move)"). With this, and the [Cascade Badge](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Badge#Cascade_Badge "Badge"), the tree blocking the way to Vermilion Gym can be easily cut through, and [Lt. Surge](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lt._Surge "Lt. Surge"), a Gym Leader specializing in [Electric-types](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Electric_\(type\) "Electric (type)"), can be challenged. From here, [Route 11](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_11 "Kanto Route 11") beckons, as does [Diglett's Cave](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Diglett%27s_Cave "Diglett's Cave"), through which is the only way to get back to [Route 2](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_2 "Kanto Route 2"), and a second HM, containing [Flash](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Flash_\(move\) "Flash (move)") held by one of [Professor Oak](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Professor_Oak "Professor Oak") 's aides on [Route 2](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_2 "Kanto Route 2"). The player takes a brief detour to Pewter City's museum's back entrance which was previously blocked due to a Cut-able tree, which can now easily be bypassed. The player obtains the [Old Amber](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Old_Amber "Old Amber") in the museum. Heading back to Diglett's Cave, and to Vermilion, the player must go to Cerulean and to the east, onto [Route 9](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_9 "Kanto Route 9") and towards the [Rock Tunnel](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rock_Tunnel "Rock Tunnel").

Rock Tunnel, a still undeveloped natural tunnel between the sections of [Route 10](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_10 "Kanto Route 10"), is pitch black inside; for this reason, [Flash](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Flash_\(move\) "Flash (move)") is recommended, but not required, for navigation of it. Finally reaching [Lavender Town](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lavender_Town "Lavender Town"), the only town in Kanto without a Pokémon Gym besides Pallet, there is not much to do; the local [Pokémon Tower](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Tower "Pokémon Tower") is haunted. From here, [Route 8](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_8 "Kanto Route 8") leads to Saffron City, but it again must be bypassed by way of another [Underground Path](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Underground_Path_\(Kanto_Routes_7%E2%80%938\) "Underground Path (Kanto Routes 7–8)"), which has its other entrance on [Route 7](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_7 "Kanto Route 7"), on the west side of Saffron. [Celadon City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celadon_City "Celadon City"), the home of the fourth Gym which specializes in [Grass-type](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Grass_\(type\) "Grass (type)") Pokémon, is just a short walk further. Like the Vermilion Gym, the Celadon Gym also has a small tree blocking the way to its entrance, and an old man outside.

The [Rocket Game Corner](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celadon_Game_Corner "Celadon Game Corner") in Celadon is not what it appears to be. In fact, the Game Corner itself is merely the above-ground portion of a sprawling underground complex: the [Rocket Hideout](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Team_Rocket_Hideout "Team Rocket Hideout"). The player runs into Jessie and James for a second time and then meets the Team Rocket boss, Giovanni, who after his defeat flees, leaving behind a Silph Scope. A Silph Scope is required to fully navigate the Pokémon Tower inside of Lavender Town that the player encountered earlier.

After this, the Pokémon Tower can be navigated, and the ghosts haunting it are revealed to be [Gastly](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gastly_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Gastly (Pokémon)") and [Haunter](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Haunter_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Haunter (Pokémon)"). In front of the stairs to the final floor, blocking the way, is also a final spirit, that of a deceased [Marowak](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Marowak_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Marowak (Pokémon)") that was killed by Team Rocket when they captured her child. Making it all the way to the top reveals [Mr. Fuji](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mr._Fuji "Mr. Fuji") held hostage by Jessie and James, who will leave when they are defeated. Fuji gives away the [Poké Flute](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Flute "Poké Flute"), and with that, the [Snorlax](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Snorlax_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Snorlax (Pokémon)") blocking [Route 11](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_11 "Kanto Route 11") and [Route 16](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_16 "Kanto Route 16") can finally be moved away. Another HM, containing [Fly](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fly_\(move\) "Fly (move)"), can be obtained easily by cutting away a tree blocking the northern section of Route 16.

Now the player is presented with a choice of how to get to [Fuchsia City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fuchsia_City "Fuchsia City"). Traveling down either way the Snorlax are blocking, a faster way via Routes 16, [17](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_17 "Kanto Route 17"), and [18](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_18 "Kanto Route 18") on [Cycling Road](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_17 "Kanto Route 17"), or down the [Silence Bridge](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_12#Silence_Bridge "Kanto Route 12") of Routes [12](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_12 "Kanto Route 12"), [13](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_13 "Kanto Route 13"), [14](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_14 "Kanto Route 14"), and [15](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_15 "Kanto Route 15"), inevitably brings one to the southernmost city in continental Kanto, [Fuchsia City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fuchsia_City "Fuchsia City"), home of [Koga](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Koga "Koga") of the [Poisonous](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Poison_\(type\) "Poison (type)") Fuchsia Gym and the [Kanto Safari Zone](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Safari_Zone "Kanto Safari Zone"). The Safari Zone is currently running a contest: the person to reach a specific rest house first will win yet another HM, containing [Surf](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Surf_\(move\) "Surf (move)"). Finding the [Safari Zone Warden](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Baoba "Baoba") 's [Gold Teeth](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gold_Teeth "Gold Teeth") also will have him reward the player with the final of Kanto's HMs, containing [Strength](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Strength_\(move\) "Strength (move)").

The player then goes back to either [Celadon City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celadon_City "Celadon City") or [Lavender Town](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lavender_Town "Lavender Town"), encountering the other [Snorlax](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Snorlax_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Snorlax (Pokémon)") on the way back. After stopping off at the [Celadon Department Store](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celadon_Department_Store "Celadon Department Store") and buying a drink at a [vending machine](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Vending_machine "Vending machine"), Saffron City can finally be entered. However, Team Rocket is guarding almost every door in the city, including that of the local Pokémon Gym! One of the open buildings, however, is the unofficial Fighting-type Pokémon Gym. After the player defeats the Fighting Dojo, he is entitled to either a [Hitmonchan](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Hitmonchan_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Hitmonchan (Pokémon)") or a [Hitmonlee](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Hitmonlee_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Hitmonlee (Pokémon)"). The city's centerpiece building, [Silph Co.](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Silph_Co. "Silph Co.")'s headquarters, has also been infiltrated by the organization, and at the top, waiting in the boardroom guarded by Jessie and James, is the Team Rocket Boss, [Giovanni](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Giovanni "Giovanni"), appearing for a second time, demanding that the president give him the [Master Ball](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Master_Ball "Master Ball") that the company had developed. After his defeat, he flees.

After Team Rocket clears out of [Saffron City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Saffron_City "Saffron City"), all buildings previously blocked are now open, as well as the Gym. The Gym Leader, [Sabrina](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Sabrina "Sabrina"), specializes in [Psychic-types](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Psychic_\(type\) "Psychic (type)"). The floor, as well, is covered in warp tiles that make it difficult to navigate. After Sabrina's defeat, the player makes his way back to Fuchsia City again and heads out to obtain the rest of the Badges.

With six Badges in hand, and five HMs in the bag, finally the player can adventure onto the open sea of Routes [19](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_19 "Kanto Route 19") and [20](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_20 "Kanto Route 20"). A short way across them, of course, is a minor obstacle: the [Seafoam Islands](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Seafoam_Islands "Seafoam Islands"). After they have been navigated through, the player can continue on Route 20 to [Cinnabar Island](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cinnabar_Island "Cinnabar Island"), home of [Blaine](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blaine "Blaine") 's [Fire-type](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fire_\(type\) "Fire (type)") Gym. There are also several more facilities on the island, including one that actually revives Pokémon [Fossils](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fossil "Fossil"). After Blaine's defeat, only the final Gym, that of Viridian City, remains.

Finally unlocked, the Gym, whose leader specializes in [Ground-types](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ground_\(type\) "Ground (type)"), is revealed to be none other than the boss of Team Rocket himself, [Giovanni](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Giovanni "Giovanni")! After his defeat, he vows to disband Team Rocket and disappears. Now with eight Badges, all that lies ahead is the [Pokémon League](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_League "Pokémon League") at [Indigo Plateau](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Indigo_Plateau "Indigo Plateau"), conveniently at the end of [Route 23](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_23 "Kanto Route 23").

The [Elite Four](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Elite_Four "Elite Four") await challengers, those who have proven themselves worthy by getting all eight Badges and making it through [Victory Road](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Victory_Road_\(Kanto\) "Victory Road (Kanto)") will face them, in order. [Lorelei](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lorelei "Lorelei"), who [trains](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_training "Pokémon training") [Ice-type](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ice_\(type\) "Ice (type)") Pokémon is first, followed by [Bruno](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bruno "Bruno"), whose specialty is [Fighting](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fighting_\(type\) "Fighting (type)"), [Agatha](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Agatha "Agatha"), whose specialty is [Ghost](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ghost_\(type\) "Ghost (type)"), and finally [Lance](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lance "Lance"), who specializes in [Dragon-types](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Dragon_\(type\) "Dragon (type)"). After defeating these four, the reigning [Pokémon Champion](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Champion "Pokémon Champion") challenges the player to a final battle, and the Champion is none other than the player's own rival! After his defeat, Oak arrives and tells the player that he won because he cares better for his Pokémon, and the player's current [party](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Party "Party") are added to the [Hall of Fame](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Hall_of_Fame "Hall of Fame").

After the credits roll, the player is back in Pallet Town. The previously closed [Cerulean Cave](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cerulean_Cave "Cerulean Cave") is now open, and the powerful [Mewtwo](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mewtwo_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Mewtwo (Pokémon)") can be found inside.

## Blurb

You've finally been granted your [Pokémon Trainer's license](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Trainer%27s_license "Pokémon Trainer's license"), and now you're on your way to becoming the world's greatest Pokémon Trainer! The shockingly-cute [Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Pikachu (Pokémon)") [tags along](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Walking_Pok%C3%A9mon "Walking Pokémon") behind [you](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Player_character "Player character") as you search the enormous world for monsters to train and evolve. Face off against [Blastoise](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blastoise_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Blastoise (Pokémon)") 's torrential water cannons. Stand strong when facing [Pidgeot](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pidgeot_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Pidgeot (Pokémon)") 's stormy [Gust](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gust_\(move\) "Gust (move)"). Develop the ultimate Pokémon strategy to defeat the eight [Gym Leaders](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gym_Leader "Gym Leader") and become the greatest [Pokémon Master](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Master "Pokémon Master") of all time!

## Changes from Red, Green, and Blue

### Aesthetic changes

- The front Pokémon sprites have all been updated to resemble how they appear in their then-current [Ken Sugimori](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ken_Sugimori "Ken Sugimori") artwork usually used in promotional images and strategy guides for the Japanese [Red and Green](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Green_Versions "Pokémon Red and Green Versions"), [Japanese Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Blue_Version_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Blue Version (Japanese)"), and the [Western Red and Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Blue_Versions "Pokémon Red and Blue Versions"), as well as some of them resembling how they appear in [*Pokémon the Series*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series "Pokémon the Series"). However, the back sprites are the same as the previous games.
- Some [Trainer](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Trainer "Pokémon Trainer") sprites were also redone. [Red](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Red_\(game\) "Red (game)") and [Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blue_\(game\) "Blue (game)") now better resemble their stock artwork for [Generation I](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_I "Generation I"), as the Pokémon sprites do. [Brock](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Brock "Brock") wears an outfit like his [animated counterpart](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Brock_\(anime\) "Brock (anime)") 's and [Misty](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Misty "Misty") 's bikini was changed to a shirt and shorts similar to those worn by [her counterpart](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Misty_\(anime\) "Misty (anime)").
- [Blaine](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blaine "Blaine") has a different overworld sprite, this time clearly showing him wearing glasses.
- [Giovanni](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Giovanni "Giovanni") uses a [Persian](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Persian_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Persian (Pokémon)") in the battle at the [Viridian Gym](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Viridian_Gym "Viridian Gym"), referencing [Giovanni](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Giovanni_\(anime\) "Giovanni (anime)") owning a Persian in *Pokémon the Series*.
- All policemen were recast as [Officer Jennys](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Officer_Jenny "Officer Jenny") and all [Pokémon Center ladies](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Center_lady "Pokémon Center lady") were recast as [Nurse Joys](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nurse_Joy "Nurse Joy") (attended by a [Chansey](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Chansey_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Chansey (Pokémon)")).
- [Jessie](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jessie "Jessie") and [James](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/James "James") have their own individual overworld sprites () and share a group battle sprite.
- [Professor Oak](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Professor_Oak "Professor Oak") has a new back sprite, as seen when he catches [Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Yellow\) "Pikachu (Yellow)") at the start of the game.
- The newly introduced [Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Pikachu (Pokémon)") sprite is used in the overworld for [the player's Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Yellow\) "Pikachu (Yellow)"). This sprite is also used as the [icon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/List_of_Pok%C3%A9mon_icons_in_Generations_I_and_II "List of Pokémon icons in Generations I and II") for any Pikachu or [Raichu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Raichu_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Raichu (Pokémon)") in the [party](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Party "Party"), instead of using the [Clefairy](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Clefairy_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Clefairy (Pokémon)") -like icon from earlier games.
	- If a Pikachu owned by the player uses [Surf](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Surf_\(move\) "Surf (move)") outside of battle, this Pikachu appears as the sprite instead of the generic aquatic sprite.
		- Some Pokémon received new sprites that show their individual appearance more clearly instead of using generic icons shared by multiple Pokémon: [Bulbasaur](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bulbasaur_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Bulbasaur (Pokémon)"), [Sandshrew](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Sandshrew_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Sandshrew (Pokémon)"), [Clefairy](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Clefairy_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Clefairy (Pokémon)"), [Jigglypuff](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jigglypuff_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Jigglypuff (Pokémon)"), [Oddish](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Oddish_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Oddish (Pokémon)"), and [Chansey](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Chansey_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Chansey (Pokémon)") (). However, much like Snorlax since the previous games, those new sprites only appear in the overworld; they still use their old generic sprites in the party.
- [Kanto](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto "Kanto") is slightly redesigned a second time, featuring different designs for doors, signposts, windows, and other minor elements.
- The [slot machines](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Slot_machine "Slot machine") in the [Game Corner](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Celadon_Game_Corner "Celadon Game Corner") have a new design, including different icons and color palettes. Since Pokémon Red, Green, and Blue also had their own slot machine designs, this is the fourth version.
- The flashing at the start of a wild Pokémon battle encounter transition (in a non-dungeon location) is slowed down, likely in response to the [Pokémon Shock](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Shock "Pokémon Shock") incident.
- In the Japanese version of Pokémon Yellow, the player character [Red](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Red_\(game\) "Red (game)") has a Super Famicom in [his bedroom](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Player%27s_house "Player's house") (as opposed to a Famicom in the Japanese versions of [Pokémon Red, Green](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Green_Versions "Pokémon Red and Green Versions"), and [Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Blue_Version_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Blue Version (Japanese)")). However, in the international versions of all [Generation I](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_I "Generation I") games, the player has an [SNES](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Super_Nintendo "Super Nintendo") instead.
- The [credits](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_credits "Game credits") of Pokémon Yellow now include [Gengar](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gengar_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Gengar (Pokémon)") as the 16th Pokémon silhouette, as opposed to the previous games with only 15 Pokémon silhouettes in the credits.

### Gameplay changes

- [Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Pikachu (Pokémon)") is the only [first partner Pokémon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/First_partner_Pok%C3%A9mon "First partner Pokémon") the [player](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Player_character "Player character") can receive, referencing the [first episode](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/EP001 "EP001"). This Pikachu [travels alongside the player](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Walking_Pok%C3%A9mon "Walking Pokémon") on-screen, outside of the [Poké Ball](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Ball "Poké Ball") —just like [Ash’s Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ash%E2%80%99s_Pikachu "Ash’s Pikachu"), who dislikes being inside one. It will also [cry](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cry "Cry") like Ash's Pikachu in [*Pokémon the Series*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series "Pokémon the Series"), with its cries now [voiced](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Voice_acting_in_the_Pok%C3%A9mon_games "Voice acting in the Pokémon games") by [Ikue Otani](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ikue_Otani "Ikue Otani").
	- This Pikachu cannot be [evolved](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Evolution "Evolution") or [released](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Released_Pok%C3%A9mon "Released Pokémon"), referencing two [certain](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/EP014 "EP014") [episodes](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/EP039 "EP039").
- The [rival](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blue_\(game\) "Blue (game)") starts with [Eevee](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Eevee_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Eevee (Pokémon)"). He will eventually have a different team based around one of Eevee's Evolutions depending on how the player fares in the first two battles with the rival (the second being optional), at [Professor Oak's Laboratory](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Professor_Oak%27s_Laboratory "Professor Oak's Laboratory") and [Route 22](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Route_22 "Kanto Route 22").
	- If the player wins both, Eevee evolves into [Jolteon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jolteon_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Jolteon (Pokémon)").
		- If the player wins at the lab and loses or skips at Route 22, Eevee evolves into [Flareon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Flareon_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Flareon (Pokémon)").
		- If the player loses at the lab, Eevee evolves into [Vaporeon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Vaporeon_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Vaporeon (Pokémon)").
- A minimal [friendship](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Friendship "Friendship") system is implemented to track how Pikachu feels about the player.
- [Gym Leaders](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gym_Leader "Gym Leader") have different teams and movesets, some based on those that appear in *Pokémon the Series*.
- [Level](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Level "Level") -up learnsets and [TM](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/TM "TM") compatibility are altered slightly for certain Pokémon. [Charizard](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Charizard_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Charizard (Pokémon)"), for example, can now learn [Fly](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Fly_\(move\) "Fly (move)") via [HM02](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/HM02 "HM02").
- Support is added for the [Game Boy Printer](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Printer "Game Boy Printer").
- If the player does not have [$](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Dollar "Pokémon Dollar") 500, they can still enter the [Safari Zone](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kanto_Safari_Zone "Kanto Safari Zone"). The player will have to pay any money they do have and will receive a number of [Safari Balls](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Safari_Ball "Safari Ball") proportional to the amount paid (but at least one). It can also be entered for free should the player keep trying.
- [Kinesis](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kinesis_\(move\) "Kinesis (move)") is given to [Kadabra](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kadabra_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Kadabra (Pokémon)") as its [signature move](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Signature_move "Signature move"). In the previous games, [Kinesis](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Kinesis_\(move\) "Kinesis (move)") was never used and the only way it might appear would be if randomly called by [Metronome](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Metronome_\(move\) "Metronome (move)").
- A new mode, "Colosseum 2", was added to the [Cable Club](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Center#Generation_I "Pokémon Center").
- The [Viridian City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Viridian_City "Viridian City") [Poké Mart](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Mart "Poké Mart") now sells [Potions](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Potion "Potion"). The NPC complaining about Potions not being sold in Pokémon Red and Blue now states that the Mart finally sells Potions again.
- In-game opponents have altered movesets where their Pokémon may use moves that aren’t exclusively learned by level-up unlike the opponent movesets encountered in Pokémon Red, Green, and Blue.

### Storyline changes

- The chain of events leading to the player obtaining their first partner Pokémon is slightly different:
	- The player now takes one step further into the [tall grass](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Tall_grass "Tall grass") before [Professor Oak](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Professor_Oak "Professor Oak") comes along. Now himself standing in the tall grass, Oak encounters a wild Pikachu and immediately catches it before leading the player back to his laboratory.
		- Instead of giving the player and [Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blue_\(game\) "Blue (game)") the choice between [Bulbasaur](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bulbasaur_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Bulbasaur (Pokémon)"), [Charmander](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Charmander_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Charmander (Pokémon)"), and [Squirtle](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Squirtle_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Squirtle (Pokémon)"), Professor Oak tells the player to take the single [Poké Ball](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Ball "Poké Ball") on his desk, containing [Eevee](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Eevee_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Eevee (Pokémon)"), but Blue shoves the player out of the way and takes the Poké Ball instead. Oak then gives the player the only Pokémon he has on hand: the Pikachu he has caught before, which, after the first battle against Blue, immediately breaks free from its Poké Ball and follows the player on foot from this point onward.
- The [old man](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Old_man_\(Kanto\) "Old man (Kanto)") in [Viridian City](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Viridian_City "Viridian City") now must be spoken to before going to [Viridian Forest](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Viridian_Forest "Viridian Forest"). He attempts to demonstrate how to catch a Pokémon, fails and leaves to get more Poké Balls from the Viridian [Poké Mart](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Mart "Poké Mart"). The player can visit the Poké Mart to ask him to repeat the tutorial.
- [Jessie](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jessie "Jessie") and [James](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/James "James") appear throughout the game, replacing Grunts from the previous games in four different places. They are involved in [Team Rocket](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Team_Rocket "Team Rocket") 's scheme and are typically found before a battle with [Giovanni](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Giovanni "Giovanni"). Though they are named "Rocket" like any [Team Rocket Grunt](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Team_Rocket_Grunt_\(Trainer_class\) "Team Rocket Grunt (Trainer class)"), they have distinct overworld and in-battle sprites.

### Missing Pokémon

The following Pokémon are not obtainable in Yellow. ## Connectivity

Players may [trade](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Trade "Trade") Pokémon between two cartridges or battle with another cartridge using a [Game Boy](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") [Game Link Cable](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Link_Cable "Game Link Cable"). To take full advantage of this feature, several Pokémon are exclusive to other Generation I games and others require trading to evolve, making trading necessary to complete the [Pokédex](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9dex "Pokédex"). The Japanese version can trade and battle with Japanese versions of [Pokémon Red, Green](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Green_Versions "Pokémon Red and Green Versions"), [Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Blue_Version_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Blue Version (Japanese)"), and Yellow; the Western versions can trade and battle with Western versions of [Pokémon Red, Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Blue_Versions "Pokémon Red and Blue Versions"), and Yellow. It can also trade with [Pokémon Gold, Silver](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Gold_and_Silver_Versions "Pokémon Gold and Silver Versions"), and [Crystal](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Crystal_Version "Pokémon Crystal Version") via the [Time Capsule](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Time_Capsule "Time Capsule"). Pokémon Yellow is completely incompatible with games from [Generation III](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_III "Generation III") onward.

Trades between Pokémon games in different languages are possible; however, a Japanese game cannot connect with a non-Japanese game without causing corruption. This is due to the fact that the games cannot automatically translate the Pokémon data from Japanese to a different language or vice versa, since neither game fully [encodes](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Character_encoding_\(Generation_I\) "Character encoding (Generation I)") both [kana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/kana "wp:kana") and the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet "wp:Latin alphabet") (only encoding one fully and the other partially). If a battle between a Japanese game and a non-Japanese game is attempted, the battle simply does not work, with the save files left unharmed.

Pokémon Yellow is compatible with the Japan-exclusive [Pokémon Stadium](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Stadium_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Stadium (Japanese)"), Western [Stadium](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Stadium "Pokémon Stadium"), and [Stadium 2](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Stadium_2 "Pokémon Stadium 2"). While link battles are not possible directly between Pokémon Yellow and the Generation II games, a player may challenge a Generation II game using Pokémon Stadium 2.

Pokémon Yellow is also compatible with the [Game Boy Printer](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Printer "Game Boy Printer"). It is possible to print Pokédex entries, the [Diploma](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Diploma "Diploma"), [Pokémon Storage System](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Storage_System "Pokémon Storage System") Boxes, [Party](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Party "Party") Pokémon, and the [Pikachu's Beach](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu%27s_Beach "Pikachu's Beach") high score.

### Colosseum 2

Yellow debuted a new link battle mode, Colosseum 2. In Colosseum 2, players can select from several battle modes:

- [Pika Cup](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pika_Cup "Pika Cup") (for Pokémon between [levels](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Level "Level") 15 to 20 only; their combined level limit is 50)
- [Petit Cup](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Petit_Cup "Petit Cup") (for Pokémon between levels 25 to 30, with a height no more than 6'07" (2 m), and weight no more than 44.1 lbs. (20 kg) only)
- [Poké Cup](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Cup "Poké Cup") (for Pokémon between levels 50 to 55 only, and the sum of all entered Pokémon's Levels cannot exceed 155; [Mew](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mew_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Mew (Pokémon)") is not allowed).

These three cups would later become standard cups in [Pokémon Stadium](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Stadium "Pokémon Stadium") (known as Pokémon Stadium 2 in Japan).

In addition, in the Japanese versions, [Blizzard](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blizzard_\(move\) "Blizzard (move)") 's chance to [freeze](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Freeze_\(status_condition\) "Freeze (status condition)") in Colosseum 2 is changed to 10% rather than the usual 30%. Blizzard's chance to freeze is always 10% in the [Pokémon Stadium series](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Stadium_series "Pokémon Stadium series") and the Western releases of the Generation I games.

### Virtual Console

The [Nintendo 3DS](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nintendo_3DS "Nintendo 3DS") [Virtual Console](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Virtual_Console "Virtual Console") release uses 3DS wireless communication as a substitute for the Game Link Cable. The Virtual Console release disables [Game Boy Printer](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Printer "Game Boy Printer") compatibility. Japanese and non-Japanese Generation I core series games do not recognize each other when attempting to link them via 3DS wireless communication.

Using [Poké Transporter](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Transporter "Poké Transporter"), all Pokémon in Box 1 can be sent from the Generation I core series games to [Pokémon Bank](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Bank "Pokémon Bank") (regardless of language), and from Pokémon Bank, they can then be moved to [Generation VII](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_VII "Generation VII") core series games.

Additionally, all of the [Game Boy Printer](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Printer "Game Boy Printer") features are disabled, although the option still appears in the PC menu: when selected, the game acts as if the printing is in progress, even though, in reality, nothing is happening.

Using [Poké Transporter](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9_Transporter "Poké Transporter"), Pokémon can be sent from the Generation I core series games to [Pokémon Bank](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Bank "Pokémon Bank"), and from Pokémon Bank, they can then be moved to [Generation VII](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_VII "Generation VII") core series games.

The moves [Blizzard](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blizzard_\(move\) "Blizzard (move)"), [BubbleBeam](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bubble_Beam_\(move\) "Bubble Beam (move)"), [Confusion](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Confusion_\(move\) "Confusion (move)"), [Dream Eater](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Dream_Eater_\(move\) "Dream Eater (move)"), [Explosion](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Explosion_\(move\) "Explosion (move)"), [Guillotine](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Guillotine_\(move\) "Guillotine (move)"), [Hyper Beam](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Hyper_Beam_\(move\) "Hyper Beam (move)"), [Mega Kick](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mega_Kick_\(move\) "Mega Kick (move)"), [Mega Punch](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Mega_Punch_\(move\) "Mega Punch (move)"), [Psychic](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Psychic_\(move\) "Psychic (move)"), [Reflect](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Reflect_\(move\) "Reflect (move)"), [Rock Slide](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rock_Slide_\(move\) "Rock Slide (move)"), [Selfdestruct](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Self-Destruct_\(move\) "Self-Destruct (move)"), [Spore](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Spore_\(move\) "Spore (move)"), and [Thunderbolt](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Thunderbolt_\(move\) "Thunderbolt (move)") had their animations changed slightly to tone down the flashing.

In the Western language releases, which are emulated in [Game Boy Color](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Color "Game Boy Color") mode, [Jynx](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Jynx_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Jynx (Pokémon)") 's battle sprites have been recolored to align with its modern design, recoloring Jynx's black skin to purple. Jynx's original design had previously been subject to [controversies](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_controversies "Pokémon controversies") due to its resemblance to racial stereotypes of African-Americans. The Japanese release is emulated in [Game Boy](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") mode—which is monochromatic—so no change was made.

The requirement for the [Pikachu's Beach](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu%27s_Beach "Pikachu's Beach") minigame is now the [Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Yellow\) "Pikachu (Yellow)") received at the beginning of the game instead of [Surfing Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Surfing_Pikachu "Surfing Pikachu").

## Differences from Pokémon the Series

The game is loosely based on [*Pokémon the Series: The Beginning*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Original_series "Original series") of [*Pokémon the Series*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series "Pokémon the Series"), but has several differences:

- [Brock](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Brock "Brock") does not surrender during the [Pewter Gym](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pewter_Gym "Pewter Gym") battle and does not accompany the [player](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Player_character "Player character") thereafter.
- [Misty](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Misty "Misty") is first encountered in the [Cerulean Gym](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cerulean_Gym "Cerulean Gym"). In *Pokémon the Series*, she had left the Gym when [Ash](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ash_Ketchum "Ash Ketchum") met her (the Gym was run by the [Sensational Sisters](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Sensational_Sisters "Sensational Sisters")). She also does not accompany the player on their journey in the game.
- [Bulbasaur](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bulbasaur_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Bulbasaur (Pokémon)"), [Charmander](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Charmander_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Charmander (Pokémon)"), and [Squirtle](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Squirtle_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Squirtle (Pokémon)") are acquired from tasks. In *Pokémon the Series*, [Bulbasaur](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ash%27s_Bulbasaur "Ash's Bulbasaur") was caught in a formal battle, [Charmander](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ash%27s_Charmander "Ash's Charmander") was rescued from heavy rain, and [Squirtle](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Ash%27s_Squirtle "Ash's Squirtle") simply followed Ash.
- [Meowth](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Meowth_\(Team_Rocket\) "Meowth (Team Rocket)") can use [Pay Day](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pay_Day_\(move\) "Pay Day (move)") and does not speak in the game.

## English title

![](https://archives.bulbagarden.net/media/upload/thumb/e/e4/Pokemon_Yellow_cartridge.png/200px-Pokemon_Yellow_cartridge.png)

An American version game cartridge of Yellow

While the game's title has become widely accepted as simply *Pokémon Yellow*, its officially recognized name outside of Japan is not entirely certain. On the boxart for most English versions, the expression *Special Pikachu Edition* replaces the series' then-current slogan *[Gotta Catch 'em All!](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gotta_catch_%27em_all! "Gotta catch 'em all!")* below the Pokémon logo, while *Yellow Version* is at the bottom, near where Red and Blue had theirs. However, the expression *Special Pikachu Edition* is repeated on the box's opening and spine.

Contemporaneous materials refer to the game as *Pokémon Yellow Version: Special Pikachu Edition*,[^8] [^9] [^10] [^11] or *Pokémon Yellow* for brevity. The instruction manual refers to the game as "the Yellow version of Pokémon", "Yellow" is one of the selectable preset names for the [player character](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Red_\(game\) "Red (game)"), and *Yellow Version* can be seen on the game's title screen.

ESRB classified the game under the title *Pokémon yellow*.[^12] The Australian Classification Board classified the game under the title *POKEMON YELLOW*.[^13] The [Canadian Intellectual Property Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Intellectual_Property_Office "wp:Canadian Intellectual Property Office") records the copyright *POKEMON YELLOW VERSION: SPECIAL PIKACHU EDITION*.[^14]

[Pokémon.com](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon.com "Pokémon.com") refers to the game as *Pokémon Yellow Special Pikachu Edition* in full, but *Pokémon Yellow Version* and *Pokémon Yellow* in general usage.[^15] *Pokémon Yellow Special Pikachu Edition* is also the title used in the [Nintendo World Pokémon Series Showcase](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Afo0sk9VNww). [Super Smash Bros. for Wii U](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Super_Smash_Bros._for_Wii_U "Super Smash Bros. for Wii U") refers to the game as *Pokémon Yellow: Special Pikachu Edition* in Pikachu's trophy description.

In the Virtual Console release, the game is referred to as *Pokémon Yellow Version: Special Pikachu Edition* on the Nintendo eShop and in the manual. Like many games, it is abbreviated on the Nintendo 3DS HOME Menu, being referred to as *Pokémon Yellow Version*.

## Reception

Like [Red and Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Blue_Versions "Pokémon Red and Blue Versions"), Yellow was also well received by the press. Like its predecessors, it received a perfect 10/10 "Masterful" rating from IGN.[^16] However, it was criticized as being "just a stopgap to help us wait for the [real sequels](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Gold_and_Silver_Versions "Pokémon Gold and Silver Versions") ".[^17] Yellow received a slightly lower average score than Red and Blue of about 85% on GameRankings. Despite this, it was the site's top rated [Game Boy](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy "Game Boy") game in the year of its release, 1999.[^18]

## Trivia

- The game's Japanese release date of September 12, 1998, was intentionally timed to coincide with the release of a movie, being released two months after *[Pokémon: The First Movie - Mewtwo Strikes Back](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/M01 "M01")*.[^19] [^20]
	- As such, it came out in Japan before the [Game Boy Color](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Color "Game Boy Color"), which was not released until October 21, 1998.[^21] This explains the lack of Game Boy Color support in the Japanese versions, with the game using a yellow palette by default when played on a Game Boy Color, [Game Boy Advance](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance "Game Boy Advance"), [Game Boy Advance SP](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Advance_SP "Game Boy Advance SP"), or [Game Boy Player](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Player "Game Boy Player"), much like the red, green, and blue palettes respectively used by [Pokémon Red, Green,](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Red_and_Green_Versions "Pokémon Red and Green Versions") and [Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Blue_Version_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Blue Version (Japanese)").
		- Pokémon Yellow was also announced and released after [Game Freak](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Freak "Game Freak") postponed [Pokémon Gold and Silver](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Gold_and_Silver_Versions "Pokémon Gold and Silver Versions"), which were originally set to come out in March 1998.[^22] Instead, [Pokémon Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Pikachu "Pokémon Pikachu") came out that year.
		- The special Pikachu Yellow model of the [Game Boy Printer](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_Boy_Printer "Game Boy Printer") was also exclusively released in Japan on September 12, 1998.[^23] [^24]
- [Pokémon HeartGold and SoulSilver](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_HeartGold_and_SoulSilver_Versions "Pokémon HeartGold and SoulSilver Versions"), which re-implemented the [walking Pokémon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Walking_Pok%C3%A9mon "Walking Pokémon") mechanic that was introduced in Yellow, were released in Japan exactly 11 years after Yellow was released in Japan.
- The two available first partner Pokémon of this game, [Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Pikachu (Pokémon)") ([player](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Red_\(game\) "Red (game)")) and [Eevee](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Eevee_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Eevee (Pokémon)") ([rival](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Blue_\(game\) "Blue (game)")), are the only Pokémon introduced prior to [Generation V](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_V "Generation V") to evolve with a [Thunder Stone](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Thunder_Stone "Thunder Stone").

### Unique content

In the [core series](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Core_series "Core series"), this is the only game with these characteristics:

- It was the second "upper version" game in the same [generation](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation "Generation") (in Japan, after [Pokémon Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Blue_Version_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Blue Version (Japanese)")).
- It has a single [walking Pokémon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Walking_Pok%C3%A9mon "Walking Pokémon").
- This is the only game with these [friendship](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Friendship "Friendship") -based qualities:
	- There is a single Pokémon with the friendship mechanic available.
		- Depositing a Pokémon in the [Storage System](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Storage_System "Pokémon Storage System") causes it to lose some friendship.
		- There are no available friendship-based Evolutions even though the friendship mechanic is present.
- There are two different types of [cries](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cry "Cry") available for the same species depending on whether the player is the Pokémon's [OT](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Original_Trainer "Original Trainer") (the partner Pikachu has a vocalized cry like in [*Pokémon the Series*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series "Pokémon the Series"), while a non-OT Pikachu still has a chiptune noise cry like in earlier games).
- The entire trio of first partner Pokémon introduced in the game's own [generation](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation "Generation") can be obtained without trades, transfers, or events.
- The [rival](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Rival "Rival") is seen preventing the player from obtaining a Pokémon (the first partner [Eevee](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Eevee_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Eevee (Pokémon)") in the cutscene at the start of the game).
- The rival's first partner Pokémon is able to evolve, but not the player's first partner Pokémon.
- The rival's team is influenced by whether he has won or lost earlier battles against the player.
- Out of all localized games, this is the only one where no [item](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Item "Item") was introduced.
	- In Japanese, this applies to both [Pokémon Blue](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Blue_Version_\(Japanese\) "Pokémon Blue Version (Japanese)") and Yellow.

### Introduced content

In the core series, this was the first game with these characteristics:

- It is heavily influenced by [*Pokémon the Series*](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_the_Series "Pokémon the Series"). This carries over into its remakes, [Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon:_Let%27s_Go,_Pikachu!_and_Let%27s_Go,_Eevee! "Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!").
- A Pokémon species is part of the Japanese title. This is also the case in the remakes.
- This was the first localized game whose boxart lacks the " [Gotta catch 'em all!](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Gotta_catch_%27em_all! "Gotta catch 'em all!")" slogan below the Pokémon logo, although this slogan was reused in [Generation II](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_II "Generation II") before it was dropped from the game boxarts altogether in [Generation III](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_III "Generation III").
- Characters introduced in *Pokémon the Series* appear in-game. This includes the [Team Rocket trio](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Team_Rocket_trio "Team Rocket trio"), [Nurse Joy](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nurse_Joy "Nurse Joy"), and [Officer Jenny](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Officer_Jenny "Officer Jenny").
- [Farfetch'd](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Farfetch%27d_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Farfetch'd (Pokémon)") can be caught in the wild, instead of only from [in-game trades](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/In-game_trade "In-game trade") like in earlier games.
- The [intro](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Game_intro "Game intro") cutscenes only feature a single Pokémon species ([Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pikachu_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Pikachu (Pokémon)") in this case).
- The intro cutscenes do not feature any human character.
- It has a Pokémon whose [cry](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cry "Cry") was influenced by the vocalizations used in animation instead of using a chiptune noise.
	- This was also the first game to have a single available Pokémon species with this kind of cry, which was also the case later for Pikachu in [Pokémon X and Y](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_X_and_Y "Pokémon X and Y").
- The player has a single predefined starting Pokémon instead of three choices of [first partner Pokémon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/First_partner_Pok%C3%A9mon "First partner Pokémon").
- The player's first partner Pokémon is unable to evolve. This is also the case in the remakes.
- A Pokémon is unable to evolve even though the current game is compatible with the evolved form.
	- In later games, this was also the case with other Pokémon such as [Spiky-eared Pichu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Spiky-eared_Pichu "Spiky-eared Pichu") **<sup><a href="https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_HeartGold_and_SoulSilver_Versions" title="Pokémon HeartGold and SoulSilver Versions">HGSS</a></sup>** and [Cosplay Pikachu](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Cosplay_Pikachu "Cosplay Pikachu") **<sup><a href="https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Omega_Ruby_and_Alpha_Sapphire" title="Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire">ORAS</a></sup>**.
- The starting Pokémon for the player and the rival have no [type](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Type "Type") advantage against each other before evolving (although this changes if the rival's first partner Pokémon evolves into [Vaporeon](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Vaporeon_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Vaporeon (Pokémon)")).
- An entire trio of first partner Pokémon is obtainable in a single saved game with no trades, transfers, or events.
- A Pokémon may be available (in this case, [Bulbasaur](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Bulbasaur_\(Pok%C3%A9mon\) "Bulbasaur (Pokémon)")) depending on the friendship value of a Pokémon owned by the player.
	- This was also the case for the [lake guardians](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Lake_guardians "Lake guardians") in the [Nameless Cavern](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Nameless_Cavern "Nameless Cavern") from [Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Omega_Ruby_and_Alpha_Sapphire "Pokémon Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire").
- Even though the [friendship](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Friendship "Friendship") mechanic is present, there are no usable moves involving this mechanic.
	- This is also the case with later games from [Generation VIII](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Generation_VIII "Generation VIII") onwards.
