*"No matter the struggles or hardships you faced... you strived to do the right thing. You refused to hurt anyone."*

— **Sans's** judgement in *[Undertale](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Undertale "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Undertale")* if the player didn't kill anyone

In games that give you a choice between using lethal or [non-lethal](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/DeliberatelyNonLethalAttack "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/DeliberatelyNonLethalAttack") force in order to overcome enemies, [the former method is usually the most convenient](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UselessUsefulNonCombatAbilities "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UselessUsefulNonCombatAbilities"). Some gamers [like to play through such games](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SelfImposedChallenge "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SelfImposedChallenge") while avoiding any killings that are not absolutely required, even if there is no real reward for doing so. In essence, the player is applying a [No-Harm Requirement](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/NoHarmRequirement "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/NoHarmRequirement") towards their entire playthrough.

Pacifist Runs can sometimes be a requirement for the [Golden Ending](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/GoldenEnding "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/GoldenEnding"), or the best ending for that scenario, as it ends up with [all of the cast surviving](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/EverybodyLives "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/EverybodyLives").

Naturally, any bosses encountered in these kinds of games are almost always [skippable](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SkippableBoss "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SkippableBoss") in one way or another.

The inverse of this is the Genocide Run, in which the player's task is to kill everything that moves. This is only possible if enemies are limited in number and don't [respawn](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RespawningEnemies "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RespawningEnemies"), hardly meaningful if fighting every enemy is a practical or absolute requirement, and rarely acknowledged in games that meet both of these requirements.

Also see [Thou Shalt Not Kill](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ThouShaltNotKill "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ThouShaltNotKill"), a super-trope of sorts. [Stealth Based Games](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthBasedGame "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthBasedGame") and [Stealth Based Missions](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthBasedMission "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthBasedMission") often feature this trope alongside [Stealth Run](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthRun "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthRun"). When the game scolds you for not taking a more peaceful option, see [What the Hell, Player?](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WhatTheHellPlayer "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WhatTheHellPlayer") Can be related to [Video Game Caring Potential](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/VideoGameCaringPotential "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/VideoGameCaringPotential"). The [Badass Pacifist](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BadassPacifist "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BadassPacifist") and [Technical Pacifist](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist") attempt this in real life.

## Examples where a game recognizes pacifist runs:

open/close all folders

4X

- *[Civilization](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Civilization "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Civilization")*:
	- The Culture, Diplomatic, and (to a certain extent) Space Race victories can be achieved without a single battle (although the higher levels will manipulate you into conflicts with other civs). In the first three installments, "Always Peace" (shooting for one of these victories, but not being allowed to go to war) is a [Self-Imposed Challenge](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SelfImposedChallenge "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SelfImposedChallenge"), but since *[Civilization IV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CivilizationIV "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CivilizationIV")*, it's also an official game option. Taking things even further, in a story now lost to the archives, someone once won a game of *[Civilization III](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CivilizationIII "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CivilizationIII")* without building a single military unit. This is a significantly more impressive achievement than just "never fight any battles" — the AI is able to recognise weakly defended targets and attack them opportunistically. It was a diplomatic victory, if you're wondering.
		- In *[Civilization V](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CivilizationV "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/CivilizationV")*, it is *theoretically* possible to win by using a city-state to do your "dirty work", at least at a low difficulty level. If another civ has wiped out the others apart from you and attacks a city-state, you can supply the city with army units as gifts. If they manage to take the opposing civ's capital, you win. However, since a city-state usually will only attack a nearby enemy city, you'll have to hope you're lucky and this city ends up being the enemy capital, as they'll sue for peace if they lose an ordinary city.
- *[Endless Space](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/EndlessSpace "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/EndlessSpace")*:
	- The original game encourages this with an achievement that is rewarded for winning a game without ever placing a weapons module on a ship (bar the [Starting Units](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StartingUnits "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StartingUnits")). However, due to either an oversight or [intentional exception](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist"), planetary invasion modules don't count as weapons, allowing Amoeba ships [covered in defenses but with no "weapons"](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StoneWall "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StoneWall") to invade and annex other empires' planets.
		- *[Endless Legend](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/EndlessLegend "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/EndlessLegend")* offers an achievement for winning the game as the Drakken (a faction which favors diplomacy) without ever being at war with another player.
- *[Stellaris](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Stellaris "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Stellaris")* has the Pacifist and Fanatical Pacifist ethos. The first level locks you into liberation wars and defensive wars (you can only wage war to make your war-goal planets into a new nation that follows your ethos, stop atrocities like purges, humiliate the target empire, or to defend yourself). Fanatical Pacifists can only wage defensive wars, which means that someone must declare war on them; they can't be the aggressor for *any* reason. In exchange for taking on this handicap, Pacifist empires can support a larger number of people without running into managerial issues, have a boost to the stability of their planets (because their people are more inclined to live peacefully with each other), and throw Peace Festivals to make their people happy.

Action Adventure

- Players have found ways to complete certain *[Assassin's Creed](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/AssassinsCreed "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/AssassinsCreed")* games with a minimum of blood on their hands. One standout example is *[Assassin's Creed: Odyssey](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/AssassinsCreedOdyssey "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/AssassinsCreedOdyssey")*, where the player's kill score <sup>note</sup> can be kept at near zero by letting the [misthios's](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/PlayerCharacter "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/PlayerCharacter") [Animal Companion](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AnimalCompanion "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AnimalCompanion") Ikaros do most of the necessary killing for them. In situations where this doesn't work, like in conquest battles, paralyzing arrows can be used to neutralize enemies nonlethally. Players on a [New Game Plus](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/NewGamePlus "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/NewGamePlus") run can also equip the legendary Pilgrim armor set to make themselves invisible indefinitely and just sneak past the majority of enemies in the game.
- *[inFAMOUS: Second Son](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfamousSecondSon "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/InfamousSecondSon")*: It is entirely possible to make it through almost the whole game without killing a single person yourself, relying entirely on subduing people. The exception being when Reggie dies. It's impossible to subdue anyone during that scene. Delsin is so enraged that he obliterates every trooper in his way. It can start to bleed into [Technical Pacifist](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist"), though.
- The Gandhi bonus in the first two games of the *[The Journeyman Project](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TheJourneymanProject "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TheJourneymanProject")* series is awarded if you defeat your enemies with non-lethal force, which gives you extra points. It's possible to complete a game this way too.
- *[The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TheLegendOfZeldaALinkToThePast "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TheLegendOfZeldaALinkToThePast")* makes this easier than other games in [the series](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/TheLegendOfZelda "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/TheLegendOfZelda") because of two factors: The [Magic Cape item](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/Invisibility "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/Invisibility"), and the fact that the game doesn't require you to [Kill Enemies to Open](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/KillEnemiesToOpen "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/KillEnemiesToOpen") the way out as much as other games in the series.
- It is possible to beat *[Metroid](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Metroid1 "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Metroid1")* and have the only enemy you defeat be [Mother Brain](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/FinalBoss "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/FinalBoss"). The main obstacle comes in the form of missile ammo: Unlike later games, which have recharge stations for energy and ammunition, the [Nintendo Entertainment System](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Platform/NintendoEntertainmentSystem "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Platform/NintendoEntertainmentSystem") original only gives you these through item drops from defeated enemies. Since missiles are absolutely required to get through the final part of the game and defeat Mother Brain, you will not only need to get pretty much every missile expansion in the game (as you no longer have access to the massive 75 missile upgrades that defeating Kraid and Ridley nets you), but you also can't afford to waste a single one before reaching [Tourian](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TheVeryDefinitelyFinalDungeon "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TheVeryDefinitelyFinalDungeon"). In addition, because some missile expansions are behind doors that require missiles themselves, you have to use a glitch to enter those rooms.

Action Game

- It is possible in *[Horizon Zero Dawn](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/HorizonZeroDawn "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/HorizonZeroDawn")* to wear outfits optimized for stealth, and the varied landscape offers numerous opportunities to sneak around without alerting machines that lack the radar-sensing functionality (and even the ones with radar don't engage it all the time). As a result, once Aloy has the ability to override basic machines like Watchers and Striders, it is possible to combine stealth with the override ability to take advantage of the tendency of non-overridden machines to attack the overridden one. Consequently, aside from storyline-mandated fights against machines, it is possible to go through the entire game without killing more machines than necessary.
- Truly skilled (and crazy) runners will do this with *[Ninja Gaiden (NES)](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/NinjaGaidenNES "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/NinjaGaidenNES")*, the game that is the epitome of [Nintendo Hard](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/NintendoHard "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/NintendoHard"). Only the bosses are allowed to be killed, and since most regular enemies die in one blow, a single errant button press will invalidate the run. If you want to see jaw-dropping runs, check them out.
- In *[Pharaoh's Tomb](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/PharaohsTomb "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/PharaohsTomb")*, you only have to kill two monsters in the first part. You do get a reward though: You have a maximum of five spears, and getting a sixth gives you a lot of points.
- In *[Teppoman](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Teppoman "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Teppoman")*, if you run very fast and jump over enemies, you can freak out the AI, skip most enemies, and rescue all hostages in the first part, but the second part is impossible to do, because the game treats destroying the turret as a kill.

- In *[Quest for Glory](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGlory "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGlory")*, the Thief can make it through the original *[Quest for Glory](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryI "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryI")*, *[Quest for Glory II](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryII "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryII")*, and *[Quest for Glory III](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryIII "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryIII")* only killing two [Big Bads](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BigBad "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BigBad"). *[Quest for Glory IV](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryIV "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryIV")* also requires you to kill two [Eldritch Abominations](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/EldritchAbomination "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/EldritchAbomination"), a wraith, and the [Big Bad](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BigBad "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BigBad"). *[Quest for Glory V](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryV "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/QuestForGloryV")* does not really allow for a pacifist run, shifting as it did to a more traditional hack-and-slash action game.

Augmented Reality Game

- There are three ways to gain [AP](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ExperiencePoints "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ExperiencePoints") in *[Ingress](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Ingress "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Ingress")*: building portal infrastructure such as resonators and portal links, hacking enemy portals, and attacking enemy portals with weapon items. It is possible to achieve [Level 8](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/Cap "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/Cap") without ever using weapons, capturing a neutral portal, or hacking an enemy portal — making links between co-aligned portals and making triangles out of portal links to generate control fields yield large quantities of AP.

Driving Game

- In *[Bump 'n' Jump](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/BumpNJump "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/BumpNJump")*, the objective is generally to run as many other cars off the road as you can without crashing yourself. However, you get *substantial* bonus points if you complete a level without running anyone off the road.

Fighting Game

- *[Super Smash Bros.](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/SuperSmashBros "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/SuperSmashBros")*:
	- *[Super Smash Bros. 64](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/SuperSmashBros64 "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/SuperSmashBros64")* has a bonus ("Pacifist") for clearing a stage without even attempting an attack. Given how many stages have level hazards, this isn't as difficult as it sounds to obtain.
		- In *[Super Smash Bros. Melee](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/SuperSmashBrosMelee "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/SuperSmashBrosMelee")*, this bonus still exists and is joined by another bonus called "Switzerland", which requires not being attacked as well (complete neutrality). Yet another bonus is called "Peaceful Warrior", which lets you attack an enemy but not KO them. And all of these are necessary for [100% Completion](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/HundredPercentCompletion "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/HundredPercentCompletion"). There are [some AI quirks](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WinsByDoingAbsolutelyNothing "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/WinsByDoingAbsolutelyNothing") in certain stages that help with getting these.

First-Person Shooter

- Although most of *[BioShock Infinite](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/BioShockInfinite "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/BioShockInfinite")* requires guns blazing and kill-counts in the hundreds to complete the game, Episode 2 of the *Burial at Sea* [DLC](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/DLC "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/DLC") becomes a [Stealth-Based Mission](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthBasedMission "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/StealthBasedMission"), especially at the beginning, as Elizabeth begins the level completely unequipped. With the right use of the [Peeping Tom](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/XRayVision "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/XRayVision") and [Ironsides](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ImmuneToBullets "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ImmuneToBullets") plasmids, it is possible to finish the DLC with no kills. Some dialogue in this section hints that Elizabeth's fate depends on whether she avoids killing, which may have been based on an idea for multiple endings to the DLC, but in practice, it's just for bragging rights. [1998 Mode](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/HarderThanHard "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/HarderThanHard") removes guns and the Radar Range from the game, meaning that Elizabeth can only eliminate enemies using non-lethal methods such as knocking them out with the Air Grabber or using the tranquilizer crossbow.
- *[Cyberpunk 2077](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Cyberpunk2077 "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Cyberpunk2077")*: It is possible to go through the game without killing, if not necessarily without fighting, as the game offers multiple ways to neutralize enemies without killing them. Blunt weapons are categorized non-lethal, and there are mods you can acquire that remove the lethality from your normal ordinance, meaning that weapons ranging from four-gauge shotguns to frag grenades will become capable of putting down enemies without killing them. Any missions that require absolutely, definitely blowing up a few dozen people are entirely optional and, at worst, lock you out of select endings. The talking "Skippy" gun pokes fun at choosing to fight nonlethally, as you're still brutalizing enemies pretty badly either way.
- *[Deus Ex Universe](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/DeusExUniverse "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/DeusExUniverse")*:
	- *[Deus Ex](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusEx "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusEx")*:
		- Only two characters *have* to be fought in order to complete the game (Anna Navarre and Howard Strong); neither has to be killed, and both can be circumvented. The first can be injured to the point that they'll retreat and open the door they guard <sup>note</sup> , and the second can be knocked unconscious and is also quite willing to blow themselves up for you due to poor AI. The rest can be stunned or [circumvented](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SkippableBoss "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SkippableBoss") in various fashions. The game encourages pacifism in the first level by having characters react positively to you if you don't slaughter everything in sight, but after that, there's no longer any explicit encouragement, and it's strictly a matter of pride (although you *will* get bitched out for slaughtering civilians). Two of the endings, however, require the death of the [Big Bad](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BigBad "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BigBad"). One ending is explicit, as the player presses a button that causes him to blow up quite violently, whereas in the other, he dies as a result of the player blowing up the final level with everyone in it. In the third ending, he seemingly lives.
				- *[The Nameless Mod](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TheNamelessMod "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TheNamelessMod")* lets you avoid all enemies, including bosses. With some endings, you can finish the game as an [Actual Pacifist](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ActualPacifist "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ActualPacifist").
				- *[2027](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TwentyTwentySeven "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/TwentyTwentySeven")* also allows this. However, you will not be able to complete the final Judician mission.
		- *[Deus Ex: Invisible War](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusExInvisibleWar "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusExInvisibleWar")* picks this up, as even plot-critical deaths are optional. The benefits manifest differently depending on which faction wants them dead and how their death affects gameplay (keeping one person alive grants access to a weapon that you won't seriously need till the end), but ultimately, there are no required kills. A hidden bonus is that the wrap party has a lot more patrons the fewer people you kill. Only one of the game's four endings allows for a complete pacifist playthrough, though, as the other three require you to kill various faction leaders in the climax.
		- *[Deus Ex: Human Revolution](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusExHumanRevolution "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusExHumanRevolution")*, like its predecessors, can be played in a pacifist manner, by circumventing enemies or knocking them out with gas grenades, tasers, tranquilizers, and fists; except four forced boss battles. Unlike the first two games though, the bosses can't be circumvented, so you have to fight and kill them (even in the [Updated Re-release](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UpdatedReRelease "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UpdatedReRelease"), you can't actually prevent their deaths due to conventions). There is however an achievement for doing a pacifist run *other than* the boss battles. Technically, three of the four bosses can be taken out with non-lethal weapons, but they still die in the following cutscene anyway. There were plans to allow the player to spare at least two of the bosses, but this was cut due to time constraints. The Director's Cut went one step further, making the pacifist fighting methods a little easier to use in the boss fights. The base game itself encourages the pacifist run by giving bonus EXP for non-lethal takedowns.
		- *[Deus Ex: Mankind Divided](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusExMankindDivided "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DeusExMankindDivided")*: Every enemy can be taken down non-lethally, particularly since the game features additional non-lethal offensive options as well as opportunities to talk down would-be enemies. The final and only boss of the game, Viktor Marchenko, can even be defeated non-lethally provided that you can prevent him from activating [Titan Armor](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/InstantArmor "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/InstantArmor").
- One of the demo categories of *[Doom](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/Doom "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Franchise/Doom")* speedrunning site [Compet-n](https://web.archive.org/web/20140412060936/https://www.doom2.net/~compet-n/index.cgi) (which was subsequently adopted by other sites, like the [Doomed Speed Demos Archive](http://www.doomworld.com/sda/doom_sda.htm)) is [UV "pacifist"](http://doom.wikia.com/wiki/UV_pacifist), where the player must complete a level on the "Ultra-Violence" (Hard) difficulty setting without harming any monsters (monster infighting and unintentional [telefrags](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/Telefrag "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/Telefrag") are allowed).
	- Beating *[The Ultimate Doom](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Doom1993 "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Doom1993")* Episodes 1, 3 and 4 is possible in this way, but E2M8 is [Unwinnable by Design](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UnwinnableByDesign "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/UnwinnableByDesign") as far as Pacifist Runs are concerned, given that the map requires you to kill a Cyberdemon to proceed. Notably, E4M6, "Against Thee Wickedly", while technically possible to beat pacifist, took a long time to do so and ended up being an incredibly difficult challenge, with the run that achieved the challenge previously deemed impossible clocking in at *62 minutes and 49 seconds*, compared to most levels' speedruns ranging from a few seconds to two minutes at most.

This makes [Technical Pacifist](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TechnicalPacifist") characters possible, and some supplements such as the *Book of Exalted Deeds* introduce character-building options to capitalize on it such as the "Vow of Nonviolence" feat.

Stop to think about that for a moment: with this rule in force, any given scenario played through could fairly trivially *become* a pacifist run, the possibility is just something that doesn't seem to as much as occur to most players. Probably because the idea of ripping the target's life-force out or disintegrating them in a [non-lethal](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsLethalAsItNeedsToBe "/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/AsLethalAsItNeedsToBe") manner is so counter-intuitive.
